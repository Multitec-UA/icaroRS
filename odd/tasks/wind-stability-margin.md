## Specs

- S1 Request (user, 2026-11-07): "La estabilidad del riel en RocketPi me da mucho más que en OpenRocket. De hecho, lo que de verdad me da RocketPi, o sea, en Icaro, con ese dato no deberían de dejarnos volar. Revisemos por qué." After the analysis, the user chose the reporting-only option: "vamos con esa" — calculate an OpenRocket-equivalent margin with the real angle of attack at rail exit and show both numbers together ("estático 5,8 / con viento 4,5").
- S2 Physics (verified on MT1-v1.7.1's stored OpenRocket simulation): OpenRocket adds Galejs body lift for every symmetric component (nose cone, body tubes, transitions): normal-force weight `K · A_plan / A_ref · sin(α) · sinc(α)` (K = `BODY_LIFT_K` = 1.1; sinc α = sin α / α), applied at the component's planform centroid, at the actual angle of attack α. A_ref = π·r_ref² (r_ref = rocket radius). The RocketPy fork has no such term; its CP depends on Mach only. Evidence: OpenRocket CP 1.030 m at rail exit (α = 0.110 rad), 1.065 (0.053), 1.090 (0.020), 1.105 at α ≈ 0.002 — equal to icaro's 1.105 m. OpenRocket source: https://github.com/openrocket/openrocket/blob/release-23.09/core/src/net/sf/openrocket/aerodynamics/barrowman/SymmetricComponentCalc.java (`BODY_LIFT_K`, `getLiftCP`); Niskanen thesis §3.2.2.
- S3 Wind margin definition: at rail exit (time `out_of_rail_time`, Mach of `out_of_rail_velocity`), CP_wind = (Σ surfaces CNα·x_cp + CN_body·x_body) / (Σ surfaces CNα + CN_body), where the surfaces' CNα and x_cp are RocketPy's own (`rocket.cp_position` components at that Mach) and CN_body = K · A_plan_total / A_ref · sin²α / α summed per segment at each segment's planform centroid; margin_wind = (CP_wind − CG(t)) / (2·r_ref) in calibres, same sign convention as RocketPy's `out_of_rail_stability_margin` for the rocket's coordinate system. α = the flight's angle of attack at rail exit (RocketPy `Flight.angle_of_attack`, degrees → radians). With α = 0 the wind margin equals the static margin exactly.
- S4 Body geometry: the converter exports the stage-level body segments in `parameters.json` as a new optional block `body_segments`: list of `{kind: "nosecone"|"bodytube"|"transition", name, position (m from nose tip, front), length, fore_radius, aft_radius}` in axial order (nose cone: fore 0, aft = base radius; tube: fore = aft = radius; transition: its two radii). Planform per segment: trapezoid area `length · (fore_radius + aft_radius)` and centroid from the front `length · (fore_radius + 2·aft_radius) / (3·(fore_radius + aft_radius))`; for the nose cone OpenRocket integrates the real shape — use the shape's exact planform where cheap (ellipsoid: area π·r·L/2, centroid 4L/(3π)·… derive and test; otherwise the trapezoid approximation) and say which shapes are exact. Exports without `body_segments` (stored before this change) fall back to: nose cone from `nosecones` + one tube of radius r from the nose base to the aft-most of (fin trailing edge, tail end, motor nozzle), and the result says it used the fallback.
- S5 Surfaces: API `/simulate` result scalars gain `rail_departure_stability_margin_wind` (cal) and `rail_departure_angle_of_attack_deg` (deg); stored runs without them keep working. The CLI `icaro simulate` summary prints both margins when present. Web: wherever the rail-exit stability margin is shown (KPI card, results "All results", printable report), show both — static and "with wind" — with an InfoTip explaining in plain words why they differ (body lift at the angle of attack the wind causes at rail exit; OpenRocket reports the "with wind" one; at zero wind both are equal). en + es.
- S6 Validation: MT1-v1.7.1 (fixture to add under `packages/rocketserializer/tests/acceptance/MT1--2026/v1.7.1/`, copied from `~/Downloads/MT1-v1.7.1.ork`), simulated with a manual wind reproducing OpenRocket's stored conditions (2 m/s; read direction/inclination from the .ork simulation conditions) must give a wind margin within 0.2 cal of OpenRocket's 4.54 cal; with zero wind it equals the static 5.79 cal.
